# Narrative Frameworks Playbook

> **Purpose**: Equip the DesignAdvisor and human authors with proven narrative structures
> for pitch decks, investor materials, and customer outreach documents. Covers the Loop
> Method, Pyramid Principle, and SCR — with worked examples for Aigis-style B2B pitches.
>
> **Authority sources**: Preston Chin (@prestonchin) storytelling framework (Instagram Reel
> `DW5Zg00DBze`, Apr 2026), Barbara Minto's Pyramid Principle, consulting narrative patterns.

## 1. The Loop Method (Preston Chin)

### What it is

A storytelling structure where the presenter **opens a tension loop** (raises a question or
reveals a problem), **layers in additional loops** to deepen urgency, then **closes loops in
reverse order** — resolving the deepest issue first, then each outer layer.

The whiteboard schematic: concentric rings, each ring = one open loop. The narrative
travels inward (opening loops) and then outward (closing them), with the solution
placed at the innermost point.

```
  Open Loop 1: Big market pain / macro problem
    Open Loop 2: Why existing solutions fail
      Open Loop 3: The specific unmet need
        ─────────────────────────────────────
        CORE REVEAL: Your product / solution
        ─────────────────────────────────────
      Close Loop 3: Proof the specific need is met
    Close Loop 2: Why you are different from existing solutions
  Close Loop 1: Scale of the opportunity / return
```

### Why it works

- **Tension sustains attention**: An open loop is a question the brain wants answered.
  Opening multiple loops before answering any of them creates a "debt" the audience
  wants repaid — they stay engaged to get the resolution.
- **Reversal builds satisfaction**: Closing in reverse order (deepest first) feels
  intellectually satisfying — each closure "earns" the next.
- **Avoids the "so what" problem**: Conventional pitch decks open with the product.
  The loop method delays the product reveal until the audience is primed to care.

### Failure modes

- **Opening too many loops** (4+): Audience loses track of what was opened. Cap at 3.
- **Closing in the wrong order**: Closing outer loops before inner ones breaks the logic.
- **Under-opening loop 1**: If the macro pain isn't visceral, the audience has no stake
  in the resolution. The outer loop must land emotionally.

## 2. Loop Method Applied to B2B Customer Decks

### Structure for a product/capability deck to a prospective client

The goal is to move the reader from "I don't know why I need this" to
"I need to try this immediately."

```
Slide 1   TITLE — The hook question (not a claim, a question)
Slide 2   LOOP 1 OPEN — The macro pain: cost/time/quality problem they live with
Slide 3   LOOP 2 OPEN — Why current tools fail at this specific problem
Slide 4   CORE REVEAL — What you do, how it works (pipeline / architecture)
Slide 5   LOOP 2 CLOSE — Proof: outputs, samples, comparison to status quo
Slide 6   LOOP 1 CLOSE — The offer: specific, simple, low-risk entry path
Slide 7   CTA — One action, one person to contact
```

**Worked example — Aigis customer deck (Strategic Partner path)**:

| Slide | Loop | Content |
|-------|------|---------|
| 1 | — | "What if your next VDR took 3 days, not 6 weeks?" |
| 2 | L1 open | E&P M&A: 2,000+ deals/year, £100k–500k advisory per deal, 80% of analyst time on formatting |
| 3 | L2 open | Generic AI tools (ChatGPT, co-pilots) don't know what to look for in a reservoir report or a JOA |
| 4 | Reveal | Aigis: 7-agent pipeline purpose-built for upstream VDR — Production, Reserves, Finance, Legal, Risk, Q&A, Report |
| 5 | L2 close | Sample output: Corsair DD report, Ascari report — show the actual output quality |
| 6 | L1 close | Strategic Partner path: 6 months free access on your live deals → £15k/month after that |
| 7 | CTA | "Run it on your next deal. No commitment." + contact + QR |

**Critical design rules for the customer deck**:
- Slide 2 (L1 open) must use a **concrete number** the reader recognises from their own P&L.
  "£200k per deal in advisory fees" is more visceral than "expensive."
- Slide 4 (Reveal) should show the pipeline as a **visual process diagram**, not bullet points.
  The 7 agents should be named and shown as a flow.
- Slide 5 (L2 close) must show **actual output**, not a description of output. A screenshot
  of a real DD report section converts better than any copy.
- Slide 6 (L1 close) must make the offer feel **low risk**. "6 months free" removes the
  price objection entirely — the only remaining barrier is time.

## 3. Loop Method Applied to Investor Decks

### Structure for a seed/Series A fundraising deck

The goal is to move an investor from "interesting" to "I need to be in this."

```
Slide 1   TITLE — Company name, tagline, round details
Slide 2   LOOP 1 OPEN — Market size: the $Xbn problem, growing YoY
Slide 3   LOOP 2 OPEN — Why nobody has built the right solution (domain + AI barrier)
Slide 4   LOOP 3 OPEN — The specific unmet need: what buyers want but can't get
Slide 5   CORE REVEAL — The product: architecture, differentiation, moat
Slide 6   LOOP 3 CLOSE — Traction: pilots, playtester feedback, revenue pipeline
Slide 7   LOOP 2 CLOSE — Why we can build this (team + IP + technical edge)
Slide 8   LOOP 1 CLOSE — The return: market sizing, exit comps, use of funds
Slide 9   ASK — Round size, valuation, timeline, lead investor slot
```

**Worked example — Aigis investor deck**:

| Slide | Loop | Content |
|-------|------|---------|
| 1 | — | Aigis Analytics · AI-native upstream M&A due diligence · Seed round |
| 2 | L1 open | 2,000+ E&P deals/year globally, £100k–500k advisory per deal = $4bn+ TAM. Growing with energy transition |
| 3 | L2 open | No purpose-built AI tool exists. Building one needs both petroleum engineering domain knowledge AND LLM engineering — almost nobody has both |
| 4 | L3 open | Buyers want: reservoir + commercial + legal synthesis in one report, not three separate consultants who don't talk to each other |
| 5 | Reveal | 7-agent Aigis pipeline: Argus (VDR indexing) → Ledger (financials) → Quant (reserves) → Scribe (legal) → Conductor (synthesis) → Canvas (report). MAKOR hallucination-grounding layer. Patent filed. |
| 6 | L3 close | 22 active playtesters: Ithaca Energy, DNO ASA, Kistos, Carlingford, Gneiss Energy, Lambert Energy. 3 revenue pilots in progress. |
| 7 | L2 close | Team: petroleum engineering + LLM + M&A advisory backgrounds. UK patent filed (Family A). Defensible technical moat. |
| 8 | L1 close | Path to £3M ARR at 20 clients × £15k/month. Exit comps: Wood Mackenzie (£2.8bn), Enverus ($2bn). 10–15× return case. |
| 9 | Ask | Raising £Xm at £Ym valuation. Deploy: sales (40%), IP filing (30%), engineering (30%). Close by [date]. |

**Critical design rules for the investor deck**:
- Slide 2 (L1 open) must **size the market bottom-up**, not top-down. "2,000 deals × £200k avg = £400m UK/Europe addressable" is more credible than "$4bn TAM (Gartner)."
- Slide 5 (Reveal) should show the **MAKOR grounding layer** as a key moat — this is what makes Aigis defensible against generic AI tools.
- Slide 6 (L3 close) — 22 playtesters is strong early traction. Present as a **named list** (company names, not just headcount) to show quality over quantity.
- Slide 7 (L2 close) must name the **patent** specifically — "Patent application filed UK, Family A: [title]" — vague "IP protection in progress" is a red flag for investors.
- Slide 9 (Ask) must state one number, one timeline. Never a range.

## 4. V.G.P.S. — Self-Introduction Framework

Derived from @LisaHeidle / "Lets do this" podcast (Instagram Reel `DXZTIDrjcIL`, Apr 2026).
A structured formula for introductions in networking, pitch openers, and bio slides.

| Letter | Stands for | What to include |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| **V** | Vision | What future you're trying to build / what you believe |
| **G** | Goal | What you're working on right now to move toward that vision |
| **P** | Profile | The credentials that make you credible (brief — 1 sentence) |
| **S** | Story | The connecting thread: why you, why this, why now |

**Application to Aigis intro**: "I believe oil and gas M&A can be done in days, not weeks [V].
We're building the AI analyst that makes that possible [G]. I have 12 years in upstream
finance and 3 years building LLM systems [P]. I started this because I watched analysts
spend 6 weeks formatting data I could read in an afternoon [S]."

**Application to team slides in decks**: Each team member's bio should follow V.G.P.S.
order — vision first, credentials second. The conventional CV-first bio buries the hook.

## 5. Pyramid Principle (McKinsey/Minto)

The standard consulting document narrative structure. Complementary to the Loop Method.

```
  RECOMMENDATION / CONCLUSION (top of pyramid — state it first)
  ├── Supporting argument 1
  │   ├── Evidence A
  │   └── Evidence B
  ├── Supporting argument 2
  │   ├── Evidence C
  │   └── Evidence D
  └── Supporting argument 3
      ├── Evidence E
      └── Evidence F
```

**Key rules**:
- State the conclusion FIRST, not last. Consultants call this "BLUF" (Bottom Line Up Front).
- Each level of the pyramid must be MECE (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive).
- Maximum 3–5 items at any level. Groupings of 7+ lose coherence.

**When to use Pyramid vs Loop**:

| Situation | Use |
|-----------|-----|
| Document / memo / board paper | Pyramid — reader wants the answer first |
| Pitch deck / live presentation | Loop — reader needs to be emotionally primed before the answer |
| Email subject line | Loop (hook) + Pyramid (body) |
| One-pager | Loop (zones 1-3 open, zone 4 reveal, zones 5-6 close) |

## 6. SCR — Situation, Complication, Resolution

Classic McKinsey opening for any document. Works at document level, section level,
and individual slide level.

```
SITUATION:    The context — what is true today, what everyone agrees on
COMPLICATION: What has changed or is wrong — the tension / the problem
RESOLUTION:   What should be done about it — your recommendation or product
```

**Applied to Aigis customer opening paragraph**:
"E&P deal teams run 3–6 week due diligence processes using a combination of technical
consultants, financial advisors, and legal counsel [SITUATION]. The VDR has moved online
but the analysis is still done manually — copy-pasting from 500-page documents into
Excel [COMPLICATION]. Aigis replaces that process with a 7-agent pipeline that reads the
entire VDR and produces a synthesis report in under 72 hours [RESOLUTION]."

## 7. Design Implications for Loop-Structured Decks

These rules apply when using the Loop Method in an Inkline deck:

1. **Loop-open slides use problem-framing slide types**: `before_after`, `stat`, `kpi_strip`
   (showing the painful status quo), or `three_card` (three dimensions of the problem).

2. **The Reveal slide uses a process or architecture type**: `process_flow` (pipeline steps),
   `pyramid` (capability layers), or a custom `chart` (architecture diagram).

3. **Loop-close slides use evidence types**: `chart_caption` (output samples), `testimonial`
   (user quotes), `table` (comparison vs status quo), `feature_grid` (what you get).

4. **The Ask/CTA slide uses a closing type**: `closing` (contact details) or a custom
   `stat` with the ask amount as the hero number.

5. **Section dividers between loop phases** are optional but help orient the audience.
   Use `section_divider` only if the deck is 12+ slides.
